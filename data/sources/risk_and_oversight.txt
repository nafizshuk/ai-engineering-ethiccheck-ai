## SAF-01 | Safety

Identify one way the proposed AI feature could give an incorrect or inappropriate result in ordinary use. Look for a description of who might be affected, what happens next, and how the team would detect the problem.

Useful report evidence includes a concrete failure case, a fallback that a user can actually reach, and a test or demonstration of the fallback. A statement that the system is safe is too broad to check. If the report describes no failure handling, ask how a student could correct or recover from a misleading result.

## ACC-01 | Accountability and human oversight

Look for a named role that reviews serious or disputed outcomes. The report should explain when human review occurs and how the reviewer can correct a finding or escalate a problem. A role can be stated without naming a real individual.

A general promise of human oversight does not show how a decision can be challenged. Ask for an example showing who would receive the concern, what evidence they would inspect, and what they can change. Student-facing feedback should make the review route easy to find.

## EXP-01 | Transparency and explainability

Check whether the report identifies what the system does, which information it uses, and which limitations a user needs to understand. For a generated finding, a report passage and a supporting guideline reference let users inspect where the conclusion came from.

An explanation should connect the finding to the evidence in plain language. If the report only describes the model by name, ask how a user would verify a typical result or recognize that important evidence is missing.
